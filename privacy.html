Privacy policy

This policy describes the private Hermes Agent integration operated by Maksym Babak for his own authorized accounts.

Effective 19 October 2026

Data & purposeProcessingStorageYour control

1. Information accessed and why

Hermes accesses an account only after the account holder authorizes the connection. Its purpose is email assistance and calendar planning for the owner.

  • Gmail: account identity, message metadata and content needed for requested tasks, labels, and drafts. Access supports reading, summarizing, organizing, and drafting.
  • Personal and tutoring Google calendars: calendar identities, event titles, times, descriptions, locations, attendee details when present, and other event information returned by the Calendar API. Access supports schedule planning and authorized event management.
  • Work Google calendar: free/busy intervals. This connection requests availability access rather than event details or event modification.
  • iCloud Mail: mailbox names, message headers and content needed for requested tasks, flags, and drafts through an authenticated IMAP connection.
  • Connection data: Google authorization and refresh tokens, account identifiers, and an Apple app-specific password used to maintain approved connections.

The Gmail permission requested from Google includes the technical ability to send email. The current account adapter exposes reading, organizing, and creating drafts. The assistant's operating policy requires approval of the final message and recipient before sending. The iCloud configuration currently provides IMAP access without an outgoing SMTP connection.

2. How information is used and shared

Information is used to answer the owner's requests, summarize relevant messages, organize an inbox, prepare draft replies, identify scheduling conflicts, and manage authorized calendar events. Scheduled processing requires a separate request from the owner.

Google and Apple remain the source providers for their accounts. The private Hermes environment is self-hosted on Oracle Cloud. The current default AI provider is OpenAI. Configured delegation routes can also use OpenRouter and the model providers selected through it. When a workflow uses AI, the email or calendar content included in that task may be transmitted to the applicable configured provider for processing.

Provider processing and retention depend on the provider and account settings used for that task. The operator must select processing arrangements compatible with the restrictions below. This policy does not claim that upstream providers have zero retention.

Limited use of Google data

The operator's policy is to limit Google API data to the email and calendar features described here, in accordance with the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google data must not be sold, transferred for advertising, used for credit decisions or surveillance, or used to train generalized AI or machine-learning models. Processors handling this data must support those limits.

The integration is for personal use. It does not publish inbox contents, sell access to user data, or provide advertising features. Separate account authorizations are used to route operations; a work-mail operation is not forwarded to a personal inbox merely to combine accounts.

3. Storage, security, and retention

Original mail and events remain with their account providers. The self-hosted assistant stores connection credentials in its persistent storage with restrictive file permissions. Connections to Google APIs and iCloud Mail use encrypted transport.

Messages, event information, or derived summaries included in assistant conversations may remain in Hermes conversation history, logs, or owner-created backups until the operator deletes those records. Source data, assistant records, and backups have separate lifecycles. This setup does not introduce a new automatic deletion schedule. Existing Hermes and account-provider retention settings continue to apply.

Access tokens are renewed using refresh tokens where supported. Revoking authorization prevents further use of that connection; it does not automatically erase copies already included in conversation history or backups. Credentials and retained records can be removed separately from the assistant's storage.

4. Revocation, access, and deletion

  • Remove the Hermes Agent connection through Google Account connections for each Google account.
  • Revoke the Hermes app-specific password through Apple Account → Sign-In and Security → App-Specific Passwords.
  • Contact the operator to review or remove credentials, assistant history, exported information, and backups held in the private Hermes environment.
  • Manage original messages and events directly through their source account. Work-account access and retention also remain subject to the account administrator's controls.

5. This website

These pages explain the integration. They contain no account sign-in form, mailbox interface, analytics script, advertising script, or application tracking cookie. The hosting service may process ordinary request information such as an IP address and browser details to deliver the pages and maintain service security.

6. Changes

The operator will update this notice when the integration's access or data-handling practices change. Additional Google permissions require the account holder's consent before use.
